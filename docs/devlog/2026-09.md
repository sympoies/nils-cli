# Development log - 2026-09

## 2026-09-29 - Record closed sessions in a bounded ledger

### Result

- Every removal of a session record (CLI and daemon delete, group cleanup,
  the maintenance `remove_console_record` action, archive, and group archive)
  appends one closed record to the private `<state-dir>/board/closed-ledger.json`
  after the removal commits, whether or not the board is enabled.
- The opt-in daemon serves the ledger at `GET /board/closed/v1?since=<cursor>`
  with opaque cursors, `board-cursor-invalid` for a malformed cursor, and
  `board-cursor-expired` after pruning, a ledger replacement, or a cursor ahead
  of the head. `GET /board/v1` now carries `ledger_cursor`, read before
  records are enumerated.
- The ledger keeps the newest 256 entries that are at most 7 days old, pruned
  on every append and read. A runtime that exits while its record remains
  writes nothing; v1 never writes `exited`.

### Why / context

- Item A2 of program `agent-console-board-2026-09` under `session-board-v1`,
  so an aggregator can catch up on closes across its own downtime.
- The final record is projected before the removal, while the session's state
  is still readable, and appended only after the directory rename commits. A
  ledger failure never fails, delays, or rolls back the removal; it is logged
  content-free to the observation spool, and the aggregator later classifies
  the row as vanished.
- Corrupt or unsupported ledger content is moved aside and replaced with a new
  `ledger_id` on the next append or read. An untrusted store (symlink, wrong
  owner or mode) fails with `board-ledger-unavailable` and is never repaired.

### Evidence

- New board integration tests failed first: a CLI delete and a record-only
  removal wrote no ledger, and `GET /board/closed/v1` did not exist. They now
  pass, including a CLI delete with the board disabled served under the
  daemon's `--machine`, and a delete that succeeds when the ledger store is
  untrusted.
- Ledger unit tests cover the 256-entry and 7-day bounds, cursor order, an
  empty-ledger `next_cursor` round trip, malformed versus expired cursors,
  and replacement on read and on append. A board unit test removes a record
  between the ledger-head read and enumeration through a hook, and the
  removal appears after the snapshot's `ledger_cursor`.
- Existing archive, group archive, and group cleanup tests now assert their
  exact closed entries. Temporarily writing `deleted` for archive made both
  archive tests fail.

### Links

- Issue #1815; spec `crates/agent-session/docs/specs/session-board-v1.md`.

## 2026-09-29 - Keep the pull-request head grant through pre-grant registry writers

## 2026-09-29 - Wake idle Claude workers and recover orphaned operation leases

### Result

- The notification controller now treats Claude's `idle_prompt` completion as
  an idle composer, like a debounced Stop. `main-agent worker reenter` accepts
  a live coordinated Claude worker, and supervision reports
  `idle_guidance_wake_required` (v9 envelopes) with the exact executable
  `worker reenter` argv when an idle worker holds undelivered guidance.
- `uncertain_mutation` now returns a worker-owned
  `worker_guard_operation_reconcile` action (recovery action v9) that names the
  exact lease, instead of `worker supervise` itself.
- Full registry maintenance reclaims a lease once its safety TTL has expired,
  its runtime is unchanged, and controller evidence proves the lease's turn
  superseded with no live descendant; the lease becomes `abandoned` with
  outcome `ttl-expired-inactive`. This also unblocks the wake of an idle worker,
  since notification delivery is fenced while a lease is nonterminal.

### Why / context

- Claude never reports an authoritative idle state. About a minute after its
  last Stop it emits `idle_prompt`, which replaced the Stop as the latest
  provider event, so delivery to a worker idle for longer than that was
  deferred as `recipient-working` indefinitely, and supervision reported
  `healthy_progress`.
- An expired lease produced no renewal evidence for its whole TTL, because
  maintenance renews a lease while its own turn works or its descendant lives.
  Only the execution token could end it, so a lost token blocked the session.
  Reclaiming it only at admission would still strand an idle worker, which
  never admits again and cannot be woken while the lease is nonterminal.
- Reconcile and reclaim both rely on turn-scoped evidence. Background-subagent
  mutations that outlive their admitting turn are documented as unsupported
  under enforce coordination. No `broker proof` surface was added; admission
  replay by idempotency key is the supported recovery for a lost admission.

### Evidence

- New tests failed before the fix: the Claude waiting predicate refused an
  `idle_prompt` completion, admission refused an expired superseded lease, a
  resumed idle Claude worker was classified `claim_renewal_required`, and
  `uncertain_mutation` returned the `worker supervise` argv.

- sympoies/nils-cli#1886, sympoies/nils-cli#1881, sympoies/agent-runtime-kit#183
- `crates/agent-session/docs/specs/session-coordination-v1.md`,

## 2026-09-28 - Resume a self-blocked Main Agent worker

### Result

- New manager-only `main-agent worker resume ASSIGNMENT_ID --if-revision N
  --reason TEXT --idempotency-key KEY` performs exactly `blocked -> working`
  for a post-claim blocker. The worker then re-runs its bootstrap argv and
  re-acquires its exact assignment-derived claim, including the private
  checkout-shell grant and the pull-request head grant.
- Supervision classifies every post-claim blocked worker as
  `blocked_resume_required` (v8 envelopes) with a `worker resume` template. A
  worker that re-claimed without the grant gets a typed release next action
  from `worker-bootstrap-claim-mismatch`. A
  `claim_renewal_required` worker without any claim now gets a worker-owned
  `worker_rebootstrap` action instead of a `worker supervise` self-loop.

### Why / context

- Workers release their claim after a blocked checkpoint, and bootstrap
  refuses `blocked`, so a self-blocked worker could not even run `git status`
  and no command could move it: request-changes needed `submitted`, the
  post-claim stop path needed `working`, and cancel is pre-claim only.
- Resume shares request-changes' fences and its typed companion identity, so
  `worker reenter` works after either. A `[pre-claim:<code>]` blocker fails
  closed with `assignment-preclaim-blocked`. No record migration is needed:
  blocked assignments written by v1.29.0 resume as-is. Abandoning a lane is
  resume followed by the existing post-claim stop path.

### Evidence

- The new integration tests failed first (`unknown-subcommand`, and supervision
  returned `claim_renewal_required` with a `supervise` argv and a null
  `claim_id`). They now cover resume, re-bootstrap, and checkout-shell
  admission for released, expired, and self-reacquired claims, reenter after
  resume, the pre-claim refusal, and
  resume into `reconcile-stopped` for a stopped runtime.

### Links

- #1839; program tracker #1841; `main-agent-orchestration-v1.md` spec and

## 2026-09-28 - Title Main Agent sessions from their orchestration objective

### Result

- Retitle v3 now reads the orchestration registry at each memory refresh. A
  managed worker takes its assignment `task_summary` as its objective, and its
  controller-delivered prompts no longer enter objective memory. The
  controller of an active run pivots to the run `objective_summary` once per
  distinct run objective.
- The serve latest-prompt preview withholds a managed worker's launch prompt,
  which is the generated bootstrap instruction.

### Why / context

- Workers were titled from the bootstrap prompt, so every worker in a run had
  a near-identical title. Their preview showed that prompt, including the
  absolute path of the `main-agent` executable. The Main title never followed
  new work because the origin objective is immutable and a run start carries

  A later explicit human pivot in a Main session therefore still wins until a
  different run starts. `main-agent` delivers the launch prompt before it binds
  the worker, so the worker pivot also replaces `origin` and drops earlier
  prompt journey entries (found in review). The launch prompt is matched exactly against the
  session's own private `prompt.md`, the same evidence `main-agent` uses to
  confirm delivery. No prompt-text heuristics are involved.

### Evidence

## 2026-09-27 - Split the main-agent facade into its own crate

### Result

- `main-agent` now ships from the new `nils-main-agent` crate
  (`crates/main-agent`). `nils-agent-session` keeps the session engine, the
  orchestration registry, and the group cleanup and archive engine.

- `nils-test-support` gained `bin::required_sibling`: resolve a sibling binary

### Why / context

  crate below both serve and orchestration, and the facade split did not
  require it.
- The two packages drive each other's binaries in tests, so local-fast selects
  both whenever either changes and builds both binaries before package tests.
  CI builds both before the package-scoped canary step.

### Evidence

- Test inventory is unchanged apart from the moved paths. agent-session keeps
  its lib, integration, and remote-mailbox tests. main-agent carries the 80
  facade unit tests and four CLI contract tests. One agent-session contract test
  split into a docs half, which stays with the docs, and a CLI half, which moved.
- `main-agent capabilities`, `--help`, and both completions are byte-identical

## 2026-09-14 - Hardened the dependabot apply workflow's pull-request selection

### Result

- Both jobs in `dependabot-third-party-apply.yml` now select the bump's pull request with
  `--jq '[.[] | select(.isCrossRepository == false)][0]'` instead of taking `.[0]`, so a
  same-named branch in a fork can no longer be chosen.
- The workflow-level concurrency group is scoped to the verified commit
  (`workflow_run.head_sha`) rather than the branch.

### Why / context

- `gh pr list --head <name>` filters on the branch name regardless of which repository the
  branch lives in, and this repository is public with forks. An outside user could open a
  pull request from a fork branch named like a Dependabot branch, become the first element
  of the result, and fail the `dependabot[bot]` author check — turning the privileged

  not a privilege one: the author and head-SHA gates still hold.

  turns the whole automation into a no-op. That was verified the hard way in the sibling

  automation inert while every run stayed green.
- GitHub keeps one pending run per concurrency group and cancels the previously pending one
  when a newer run queues. With the group keyed by branch, a stale event arriving after a
  useful merge trigger could cancel it, leaving a green bump unmerged with nothing
  reporting a failure. Keying by the verified commit removes that interaction; the commit
  job's non-force ref update is what actually serializes writes.

### Evidence

  a switch to the next capacity-confirmed account before a resume, and an
  effective default account resolved and projected at launch.

### Why / context

- Count quotas bounded how many receipts a principal could retain but not how
  large they could be, so a principal could hold a small number of very large
  receipts and exhaust the store anyway. A byte budget closes the dimension the
  count quota left open.
- Rejecting at the limit rather than evicting is the important half. Evicting a
  retained idempotency key makes a later replay of that key look like a fresh
  request, which turns an overflow into a duplicate-execution bug - a strictly
  worse failure than a refusal.

## 2026-09-09 - Typed the workspace lease protocol generation

### Result

### Why / context

## 2026-09-03 - Added the target-scoped workspace lease protocol v2

### Result

- Added `workspace-lease resolve`, which classifies one exact tool execution
  into zero or more canonical repository targets and owns no durable state.
  Structured `write`, `edit`, and `str_replace_editor` mutations resolve
  exactly; read-only forms, unknown tools, and arbitrary shell programs are
  handled distinctly.
- Tiered the DSH policy groups and added an advise rule mode.

### Why / context

- v1 leased the workspace; v2 leases the targets a specific execution will
  actually touch. That is what allows two agents to work the same checkout on
  disjoint files without contending, which the coarse lease made impossible.
- `resolve` owning no durable state is the design constraint that keeps it
  safe to call speculatively: classification is a pure function of the
  execution, so it can run on every tool call without accumulating anything.
- An arbitrary shell program cannot be resolved exactly, so it is classified as
  such rather than guessed at - the honest answer being the one that lets the
  caller decide.
- The advise rule mode exists so a policy can be observed before it is
  enforced, which is how a tier gets promoted on evidence rather than on
  expectation.

### Evidence

- `556067bd` (#1600) the protocol, `6674fd7c` (#1616) the policy tiers and
  advise mode.

## 2026-09-02 - Streamed attachment uploads to disk

### Result

- Attachment uploads now enforce configurable declared and observed byte
  ceilings without buffering request bodies, publishing private temporary files
  only after a successful sync and cleaning them up on failure or
  cancellation, with coverage for streaming timing, admission ordering, exact
  limits, cleanup, and compatibility.
- Admitted `block-agent-artifact-routing` to the `agent-hook` v1 allowlist and
  added starring of history sessions.

### Why / context

- Both a declared and an observed ceiling are needed: the declared size is
  attacker-supplied and the observed size only becomes known while reading, so
  trusting either alone leaves a hole. Enforcing both without buffering means a
  hostile body is refused mid-stream rather than after it has been held in
  memory.
- Publishing only after a successful sync is what makes a partially-written
  attachment unobservable; cleanup on cancellation is what keeps a refused
  upload from leaving residue in the private temporary area.

## 2026-09-01 - Added the typed agent-hook data-policy evaluator

### Result

- Added a deterministic data-policy boundary evaluating sensitive output,
  machine-local paths, and protected roots through a strict content-free JSON
  contract, with every decision bound to the exact execution identity and
  coverage for structured, streamed, binary, and replay cases.
- Added the provider session history API with latest-context paging.

### Why / context

- The contract is content-free by design: a policy evaluator that receives the

  logs, errors, and replay records become new leak surfaces. Evaluating over
  descriptors rather than payloads keeps the boundary from being the breach.
- Binding decisions to the exact execution identity is what makes a replayed
  decision detectable rather than silently reusable.
